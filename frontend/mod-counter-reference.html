1. Executive Summary & Mathematical Foundations

A Modulo-N Counter (or Mod-N Counter) is a sequential digital logic circuit that cycles repetitively through a predetermined sequence of exactly \(N\) distinct binary states (\(0, 1, 2, \dots, N-1\)) before resetting back to its initial state (\(0\)).

Key Mathematical Properties:
1. Modulus (\(N\)): The total count of unique states traversed by the counter.
2. Frequency Division (\(\div N\)): A counter with modulus \(N\) divides the incoming master clock frequency by \(N\): \[ f_{\text{out}} = \frac{f_{\text{in}}}{N} \] The output waveform taken from the most significant flip-flop (MSB) has an exact frequency of \(f_{\text{in}} / N\).
3. Flip-Flop Requirement Inequality: To build a counter with modulus \(N\), the minimum number of binary storage flip-flops (\(n\)) is governed by: \[ 2^n \ge N \iff n = \lceil \log_2 N \rceil \]

2. Classification & Glitch Physics

Asynchronous (Ripple) Counters

  • Clocking: Only the first stage (\(Q_A\)) receives the master external clock. Subsequent flip-flops are clocked by the output of the preceding stage (\(Q_{n-1}\)).
  • Propagation Delay: Cumulative across all stages: \[ t_{\text{delay(total)}} = n \times t_{pd} \]
  • Hardware Simplicity: No lookahead gating required; extremely simple wiring.
  • Speed Limitation: Maximum operating clock frequency drops as stages are added (\(f_{\max} < 1 / (n \cdot t_{pd})\)).
  • Benchmark ICs: IC 74LS90 (Decade), IC 74LS93 (4-bit binary), CD4040.

Synchronous (Parallel) Counters

  • Clocking: All flip-flops in the counter receive the identical master clock pulse simultaneously.
  • Propagation Delay: Constant, equal to a single flip-flop plus steering gate delay: \[ t_{\text{delay(total)}} = t_{pd} + t_{\text{gate}} \]
  • Hardware Complexity: Requires combinational lookahead steering logic (AND gates or K-map minimized control networks).
  • High-Speed Operation: No cumulative ripple delay; ideal for gigahertz processors and DSP pipelines.
  • Benchmark ICs: IC 74LS160 (BCD Synchronous), IC 74LS161 / 74LS163 (4-bit Binary Synchronous).

Glitch Physics & Asynchronous Reset Dynamics

How Truncated Asynchronous Resets Work in Hardware:
To force an \(n\)-bit counter to cycle through \(N\) states (\(0\) through \(N-1\)), we decode the binary representation of state \(N\) using combinational AND/NAND gates and feed the output to the active Master Reset pins (\(R_0\)).
  1. The counter counts cleanly from state \(0\) up through state \(N-1\).
  2. On the next clock falling edge, the flip-flops momentarily switch to state \(N\).
  3. Within \(\approx 10\text{--}30\,\text{ns}\) (the internal propagation delay of the reset gating), the Master Reset triggers, forcing all flip-flops immediately back to \(0000_2\).
  4. Because this reset spike lasts only a few nanoseconds, human eyes, 7-segment displays, and standard digital peripherals never see state \(N\). Exactly \(N\) stable states (\(0\) to \(N-1\)) are observed!

3. IC 74LS90: Decade / BCD Ripple Counter Engineering Guide

The 74LS90 / 7490 is a versatile asynchronous ripple counter housed in a standard 14-pin DIP package. It contains two separate, independent internal counter sections: Section 1: a divide-by-2 (\(\div 2\)) single flip-flop stage, and Section 2: a divide-by-5 (\(\div 5\)) 3-flip-flop ripple stage.

⚠️ CRITICAL POWER PIN WARNING:
Unlike conventional 7400-series DIP-14 chips which place \(V_{CC}\) on Pin 14 and \(GND\) on Pin 7, the 74LS90 and 74LS93 use non-standard power pins: Pin 5 = \(V_{CC}\) (+5V) and Pin 10 = \(GND\) (0V). Wiring Pin 14 to power will short the clock line!

Specialized 74LS90 Truncated Circuits

Truncated Mod-6 Counter (Digital Clocks)

Used for the Seconds Tens and Minutes Tens digits of real-time digital clocks (\(0 \to 5\)):

  • Wire Clock to Pin 14; jumper \(Q_A\) (Pin 12) to Pin 1 (\(\text{CP1}\)).
  • Connect \(Q_B\) (Pin 9) directly to \(R_{0(1)}\) (Pin 2).
  • Connect \(Q_C\) (Pin 8) directly to \(R_{0(2)}\) (Pin 3).
  • Ground \(R_{9(1)}\) and \(R_{9(2)}\).
  • Operation: Counts \(0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\). When count reaches 6 (\(0110_2\)), \(Q_C=1\) and \(Q_B=1\), activating \(R_{0(1)} \cdot R_{0(2)}=1\) and resetting to 0000 in \(\approx 15\,\text{ns}\).

Truncated Mod-7 Counter (Days of the Week)

Counts 7 discrete states (\(0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\)) for weekly scheduling:

  • Reset condition is state \(7_{10} = 0111_2 \implies Q_A=1, Q_B=1, Q_C=1\).
  • Connect \(Q_A\) and \(Q_B\) into a 2-input AND gate (74LS08).
  • Connect the AND output to \(R_{0(1)}\) (Pin 2).
  • Connect \(Q_C\) directly to \(R_{0(2)}\) (Pin 3).
  • When count hits 7, all three bits trigger Master Clear instantly.

4. IC 74LS93: 4-Bit Binary Ripple Counter Engineering Guide

The 74LS93 / 7493 is a monolithic 4-bit binary ripple counter in a 14-pin DIP package. It consists of four master-slave flip-flops partitioned into: Section 1: a single flip-flop functioning as a divide-by-2 (\(\div 2\)) counter, and Section 2: a 3 flip-flop ripple chain functioning as a divide-by-8 (\(\div 8\)) counter. Unlike the 74LS90, the 74LS93 has no Set-to-9 preset pins (Pins 6 and 7 are NC).

74LS93 14-Pin DIP Package Diagram

IC 74LS93 DIP-14 TOP VIEW .-----\_/-----. (Clock B / Mod-8) | 1 CP1 CP0 14 | (Clock A / Mod-2) (Reset-0 Gate 1) | 2 R01 NC 13 | (No Connection) (Reset-0 Gate 2) | 3 R02 QA 12 | (Output Bit 0 - LSB) (No Connection) | 4 NC QD 11 | (Output Bit 3 - MSB) (+5V Power Rail) | 5 VCC GND 10 | (0V System Ground) (No Connection) | 6 NC QB 9 | (Output Bit 1) (No Connection) | 7 NC QC 8 | (Output Bit 2) '-------------'
TTL Floating Pin Rule:
In bipolar TTL technology, unconnected input pins float to Logic HIGH (1). Therefore, Pin 2 (\(R_{0(1)}\)) and Pin 3 (\(R_{0(2)}\)) MUST be actively grounded to \(GND\) to enable normal counting. Leaving them floating locks the counter permanently in reset (0000)!

Specialized 74LS93 Truncated & Cascaded Circuits

Truncated Mod-12 Counter (12-Hour Digital Clock)

Used to count hours \(0 \to 11\) in a standard 12-hour digital clock:

  • Wire Clock to Pin 14; jumper \(Q_A\) (Pin 12) to Pin 1 (\(\text{CP1}\)).
  • Reset state is \(12_{10} = 1100_2 \implies Q_D=1, Q_C=1\).
  • Connect \(Q_C\) (Pin 8) directly to \(R_{0(1)}\) (Pin 2).
  • Connect \(Q_D\) (Pin 11) directly to \(R_{0(2)}\) (Pin 3).
  • No external gates required! The internal AND gate automatically resets the chip to 0000 upon reaching 12.

Cascaded Mod-24 Counter (24-Hour Military Time)

Uses two cascaded 74LS93 chips (Lower nibble IC 1, Upper nibble IC 2):

  • Cascade: Connect \(Q_D\) of IC 1 to Pin 14 of IC 2.
  • Reset condition is \(24_{10} = 0001\,1000_2 \implies Q_{A2}=1\) and \(Q_{D1}=1\).
  • Feed \(Q_{A2}\) (IC 2 Pin 12) and \(Q_{D1}\) (IC 1 Pin 11) into a 2-input AND gate (74LS08).
  • Connect the AND output to the \(R_0\) reset pins of both ICs to clear to \(0000\,0000_2\).

5. Multi-IC Cascading Matrix & High Modulus Implementations

Higher modulus values are synthesized by cascading multiple counter stages. In ripple cascading, the most significant output bit (\(Q_D\)) of the lower stage is tied directly to the clock input of the succeeding stage.

7. Karnaugh Maps (K-Maps) & Synchronous Counter Synthesis

While asynchronous ripple counters rely on sequential clocking and reset gating, Synchronous Modulo Counters clock all flip-flops simultaneously. To design a synchronous Mod-N counter, we construct the state excitation table using JK Flip-Flops (or T Flip-Flops) and minimize the excitation expressions using Karnaugh Maps (K-Maps), treating all unused states (\(N\) to \(2^n-1\)) as Don't-Care conditions (\(X\)).

JK Flip-Flop Excitation Rule:
• Present State \(0 \to\) Next State \(0 \implies J = 0, K = X\)
• Present State \(0 \to\) Next State \(1 \implies J = 1, K = X\)
• Present State \(1 \to\) Next State \(0 \implies J = X, K = 1\)
• Present State \(1 \to\) Next State \(1 \implies J = X, K = 0\)

A. Synchronous Mod-5 Counter Design (States 0 to 4)

Requires 3 flip-flops (\(Q_C, Q_B, Q_A\)). State sequence: \(0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 0\). Unused states are 5, 6, 7 (\(101_2, 110_2, 111_2\)), which become Don't Cares (\(X\)).

K-Map for Flip-Flop A (\(J_A, K_A\))

\(Q_B Q_A\)\(Q_C\) 00011110
0 1 m0 X m1 X m3 1 m2
1 0 m4 X m5 X m7 X m6
\(J_A = \overline{Q_C}\) \(K_A = 1\) Don't-Care (5,6,7)

Minimal: \(J_A = \overline{Q_C}\), \(K_A = 1\)

K-Map for Flip-Flop B (\(J_B, K_B\))

\(Q_B Q_A\)\(Q_C\) 00011110
0 0 m0 1 m1 X m3 X m2
1 0 m4 X m5 X m7 X m6
\(J_B = Q_A\) \(K_B = Q_A\) Don't-Care (5,6,7)

\(Q_B Q_A\)\(Q_C\) 00011110
0 0 m0 0 m1 1 m3 0 m2
1 1 m4 X m5 X m7 X m6
\(J_C = Q_B \cdot Q_A\) \(K_C = 1\) Don't-Care (5,6,7)

Minimal: \(J_C = Q_B \cdot Q_A\), \(K_C = 1\)

B. Synchronous Mod-10 (BCD / Decade) Counter Design

Requires 4 flip-flops (\(Q_D, Q_C, Q_B, Q_A\)). State sequence: \(0000_2 \to 1001_2\) (\(0 \to 9\)). Unused invalid BCD states are 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 (\(1010_2\) to \(1111_2\)), which are leveraged as Don't Cares (\(X\)) in 4-variable K-maps to drastically simplify the logic!

K-Map for \(J_D\) (Flip-Flop D - MSB Input)

\(Q_B Q_A\)\(Q_D Q_C\) 00011110
00 0 m0 0 m1 0 m3 0 m2
01 0 m4 0 m5 1 m7 0 m6
11 X m12 X m13 X m15 X m14
10 X m8 X m9 X m11 X m10

Summary of Synthesized BCD Decade Counter Equations:
\[ J_A = 1, \quad K_A = 1 \quad \text{(LSB toggles on every single clock pulse)} \] \[ J_B = \overline{Q_D} \cdot Q_A, \quad K_B = Q_A \] \[ J_C = Q_B \cdot Q_A, \quad K_C = Q_B \cdot Q_A \] \[ J_D = Q_C \cdot Q_B \cdot Q_A, \quad K_D = Q_A \] Notice that when count is at 9 (\(1001_2\)), \(Q_D=1\), which inhibits Flip-Flop B from turning ON (\(J_B = 0 \cdot 1 = 0\)). At the same time, \(K_D = Q_A = 1\) resets \(Q_D\) back to 0 on the 10th clock, cleanly returning the whole system to \(0000_2\)!

8. Real-World Engineering Applications & Lab Experiments

1. Digital Quartz Clocks

Cascaded chains of 74LS90 (Mod-10 for seconds/minutes units) and truncated 74LS90 (Mod-6 for seconds/minutes tens) convert a 1Hz clock directly into 60-second and 60-minute time intervals.

2. Frequency Division & PLLs

Generates precise sub-harmonics from crystal oscillators (e.g., dividing a 10MHz oscillator down to 1MHz, 100kHz, 10kHz, or 1Hz) for telecommunication baud rates and microcontroller system clocks.

3. Memory & Address Generators

IC 74LS93 ripple counters serve as automatic sequential address generators for EEPROMs, DACs (waveform synthesizers), and FIFO memory buffers.
